# METAPHOR PHYSICS LIBRARY — LITE

## 1. Build a Metaphor in 4 Steps
1. **Physics of the problem:** clogged, thickening, brittle, pressured, dirty, rusting, drying, overheating, leaking.
2. **Everyday object with the same physics** (see §2). It must be filmable on a tabletop.
3. **Sensory detail:** crust, sludge, crack, rust, slow drip, bulge, cloudiness, steam.
4. **Clip 1 shows only the stressed state.** The fix and the product come in later clips.

## 2. Object Bank (condition physics → object)
| Physics | Objects |
|---|---|
| Narrowed / clogged flow | clear glass pipe with yellow crust · thin hose with red liquid slowing · clogged metal strainer · coffee filter overflowing |
| Thick / sticky fluid | syrup-thick liquid crawling through a funnel · honey-like sludge sealing a mesh |
| Pressure / tension | rubber band squeezing a fruit · water balloon bending a glass pane · overfilled tube bulging |
| Brittle / weak | dry twig snapping · cracked clay mask · desert soil splitting |
| Dirt / toxins | ink cloud spreading in clear water · oil film on water · dusty book page |
| Aging / oxidation | apple slice browning · rusting iron chain · tarnished coin |
| Low energy | candle burning down fast · wilting flower · battery leaking |
| Restoration (for later clips) | cloudy liquid turning clear · polished coin · flower reviving in water · ice melting into calm water |
Variety rule: do not reuse the same main object across rows of one planner. Avoid the over-used sponge and balloon unless asked.

## 3. Combination Generator (when a fresh idea is needed)
Pick one from each:
- **A – Object:** clay mask, ice block, iron chain, flower, coin, mirror, knot, stone, candle, window, glass pipe, strainer.
- **B – Force:** effervescence, desiccation, oxidation, purification, absorption, liquefaction, crystallization, vaporization.
- **C – Texture:** viscous, gritty, crystalline, oily-iridescent, chalky, porous, metallic.
Template: "[A] undergoing [B], surface [C]". Example: "glass pipe undergoing slow crystallization, surface chalky".

## 4. Clip Progression (Mandate 72)
Choose ONE strategy for the whole video and keep the same object:
- **Single object (morph):** clip 1 problem state → middle clips pressure, then intervention → last clip the same object restored. Same lens and angle to prove it is the same object.
- **Two objects (chemistry):** clip 1 the "threat" object dominates (e.g., oil on water) → middle the helper enters and acts (emulsifies, breaks) → last clip the threat is gone.
Forbidden: jumping objects between clips (balloon in clip 1, pipe in clip 2).

## 5. State-of-Matter Check (Mandate 87)
- Solid (tablet, capsule, powder): crumble, dust impact, pile up. Never pour, flow, or splash.
- Liquid / viscous (honey, syrup, oil): pour, drip, fold, swirl.
- Unknown: keep the product static; do not invent liquid physics.

## 6. Physics Vocabulary for Motion (short)
Viscous invasion (thick liquid slowly coating a solid) · desiccated fracture (fast drying with spiderweb cracks) ·
elastic strain (surface bulging against a band before release) · molecular dissolve (solid fizzing into liquid) ·
absorption (liquid vanishing into a porous surface) · emulsification (oil bursting into milky micro-droplets).

## 7. Presence in Metaphor Clips
- Object clips (especially clip 1): object only — no faces, no hands, no medical devices, no product packaging.
- Hands, product, and studio setting may appear only where the code's metaphor policy allows (e.g., after the bridge clip).
